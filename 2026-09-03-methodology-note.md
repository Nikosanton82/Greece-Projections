# Greece Macro Projections to 2030: Methodology Note

Nikos Antonakakis, UNIC Athens. Built 2026-09-03. Companion to `2026-09-03-greece-projections-dashboard.html` and the data dictionary.

## 1. Purpose and design principles

The dashboard projects twelve Greek macroeconomic variables five years ahead (2026-2030) and sets the projections against the official forecasts of the IMF, the European Commission, the OECD, the Bank of Greece and the Ministry of Finance. Three principles guided the design. First, reproducibility: every number in the dashboard is produced by four scripts from raw API snapshots, and the only manual inputs are two PDF-sourced projection tables that are flagged as such. Second, no single model: for each variable a small suite of models is estimated and combined, because the forecasting literature since Bates and Granger (1969) and the more recent evidence in Stock and Watson (2004) and Timmermann (2006) shows that combinations are hard to beat on macro series of this length. Third, honest uncertainty: every projection is a predictive density, reported as a median with 68% and 90% bands, and the pseudo-out-of-sample record of each model is shown next to the projection so the reader can judge how much to trust it.

## 2. Data

Annual data, 1995-2025 (fertility from 1990; unemployment spliced with IMF data before 2009). Sources and vintages are documented in the data dictionary. Two points matter for interpretation. The 2025 national-accounts values are Eurostat's first provisional estimates released on 2 September 2026 and will be revised with the October EDP notification. And the EC's AMECO series for TFP, potential output and the output gap embed the EC's own production-function method (Havik et al. 2014), so they are estimates rather than data; the own Solow-residual recomputation with alpha = 0.35 reproduces AMECO's TFP index to three decimals, which confirms the inputs and the share but does not make the series independent.

## 3. Model suite

### 3.1 Time-series models

ARIMA with orders p <= 2, d <= 1, q <= 1 selected by AIC on the full history (statsmodels). A mean-reverting AR(1) with intercept, whose long-run mean is the 1996-2025 sample mean; for growth rates this mean still carries the 2008-2016 depression and pulls the forecast down, which is visible in the GDP-growth panel. A local linear trend model (stochastic level and slope, Harvey 1989) for level variables such as debt ratios, fertility and population; it is deliberately excluded from growth-rate variables, where it extrapolates recent accelerations. For HICP inflation an "anchored" AR(1) in deviations from the ECB's 2% objective with no intercept, which encodes the assumption that expectations are anchored.

### 3.2 Bayesian VAR

A five-variable VAR(1) on real GDP growth, HICP inflation, the unemployment rate, real GFCF growth and the current account (1996-2025) with a Minnesota prior implemented through the dummy-observation approach of Banbura, Giannone and Reichlin (2010). Overall tightness lambda = 0.2. The prior mean of the own first lag is 1 for unemployment (first-order autocorrelation above 0.8) and 0 for the other four, so growth rates are shrunk toward white noise around their intercept. A mild sum-of-coefficients prior is centred on the last observation rather than the sample mean, so that the model is not dragged toward the crisis-era averages. Predictive densities come from 2,000 draws of the Normal-inverse-Wishart posterior with parameter uncertainty included. With thirty annual observations the BVAR is the weakest link in the suite (its unemployment path drifts upward), and the evaluation gives it the lowest weight where that shows.

### 3.3 Structural and accounting blocks

Potential output follows the EC Output Gap Working Group convention: Y* = A K^0.35 (L*)^0.65. TFP growth is the combination of the three univariate TFP models (plus the scenario shift). Capital follows the perpetual-inventory identity with depreciation calibrated on 2025 from AMECO's net stock and Eurostat's real GFCF; investment grows 4% a year in the baseline. Labour input is working-age population (EUROPOP2023 implies roughly -0.7% a year to 2030), a participation rate rising 0.3 pp a year, and a NAWRU declining 0.1 pp a year from the EC's 9.0%. Actual GDP growth equals potential growth adjusted for the closure of the output gap. The EC's 2025 gap (+2.7%) is difficult to reconcile with unemployment already at the EC's own NAWRU, so the starting gap is the OECD's +0.9% and it closes 20% a year.

Unemployment uses an Okun relation, delta u = c + beta (g - g*), estimated by OLS on 1997-2025 with AMECO potential growth (beta about -0.44, R-squared about 0.5), driven by the structural GDP and potential paths. Public debt uses the standard identity d_t = d_{t-1}(1+i)/(1+g) - pb_t + sfa_t, with nominal growth from the GDP and inflation blocks, an implicit interest rate rising from 2.3% to 3.2% by 2030 as concessional official-sector loans are refinanced at market rates, a primary balance converging from the EC's 4.0% of GDP in 2026 to the MTFSP's 2.4% by 2030 with a random-walk disturbance of 0.5 pp a year, and a stock-flow adjustment of -0.5% of GDP on average (cash-buffer drawdowns and early repayments) with a standard deviation of 1 pp. Private debt follows ratio dynamics with nominal credit growth as an AR(1) around 4% (the 2025 outturn was +7%). Population is the balance of natural change (trending from -55 thousand) and net migration (AR(1) around +40 thousand, the 2023-2025 average); the 1 January 2026 figure is already known. GDP per capita in PPS combines real growth per head with a 2.5% a year drift in the PPS price level.

### 3.4 Evaluation and combination

Each time-series model is re-estimated at every origin from 2009 to 2024 using only data up to that year and produces forecasts one to five years ahead; errors are measured against the current data vintage (a pseudo-real-time design, since revised data are used both to estimate and to score). The benchmark is the naive forecast: last value for ratios and levels, ten-year mean for growth rates. Diebold-Mariano statistics with the Harvey, Leybourne and Newbold (1997) small-sample correction test equal squared-error loss against the naive forecast; with at most sixteen origins these tests have low power and should be read as descriptive. Combination weights are inverse-MSE averaged over horizons one to three. Where a structural block exists it holds a fixed weight of 0.5, because an accounting identity cannot be evaluated by pseudo-out-of-sample forecasting without also forecasting its inputs; the time-series models share the remaining 0.5 in proportion to their inverse MSE. The combined predictive density is a mixture of the model densities in proportion to the weights (2,000 draws), not an average of point forecasts, so multimodality across models widens the bands rather than being averaged away. An equal-weight combination is reported alongside as a robustness check.

### 3.5 Scenarios

The adverse scenario lowers TFP growth by 0.7 pp a year, raises the interest-growth differential by 1 pp, adds a one-year inflation shock of 1 pp that fades over three years, lowers the primary balance by 1 pp, reduces net migration by 30 thousand a year and sets real investment growth to zero. The favourable scenario raises TFP by 0.5 pp, lowers the differential by 0.5 pp, raises the primary balance by 0.5 pp, adds 30 thousand net migrants a year and sets investment growth to 7%. The structural blocks respond through their equations; for the time-series models the same shocks are mapped through fixed elasticities (GDP growth 0.8 times the TFP shock, unemployment through an Okun ramp, debt through the differential).

## 4. Headline results (baseline combination, medians with 68% bands)

Real GDP growth settles at 1.5-1.8% (2026: 1.8, 2030: 1.6; band roughly -1 to +4.5), slightly below the IMF (1.8 to 1.6) and the Bank of Greece (1.9 to 2.0). HICP inflation stays near 3% through 2028 (band 0.5 to 5.5) and eases to 2.8% by 2030, above the IMF's convergence to 2.1%; the anchored model alone gives 2.2%, the BVAR 3.4-3.8%, and the disagreement is the main source of width. Unemployment is the clearest divergence from the institutions: the combination holds it at 8.6-9.3% while the IMF, EC and Bank of Greece see 6.8-7.9%, because the Okun block keeps unemployment flat when growth runs at potential and the BVAR drifts up, whereas the institutions build in a falling NAWRU. Public debt falls to about 118% of GDP by 2030 (band 97-148), between the IMF (115.5) and the MTFSP path (127.1), with the adverse scenario at 129 and the favourable at 111. Private debt drifts down to about 92% of GDP. TFP growth of 1.0-1.4% a year and potential growth of about 1.8% are somewhat above the EC's 0.75% and 1.7% for 2026. The fertility rate edges up from 1.24 to about 1.30 by 2030 (band 1.20-1.37), far below the 1.44 assumed in EUROPOP2023, and population declines slowly to about 10.33 million.

## 5. Limitations and next steps

The sample is thirty annual observations that include a depression and a pandemic, so every model's out-of-sample error is large and the bands are wide; this is a feature of the data, not of the method. The evaluation is pseudo-real-time on final data; a proper real-time evaluation would need historical AMECO and WEO vintages, which the IMF publishes in its Historical WEO Forecasts database and could be added. The structural weights of 0.5 are a judgment, documented and easy to change in `2026-09-03-models.py`. Natural extensions are a quarterly nowcasting layer for 2026, Bayesian model averaging with predictive-likelihood weights instead of inverse MSE, a cohort-component population model with own fertility and migration assumptions in place of the EUROPOP baseline, and a debt-sustainability fan chart in the style of the EC Debt Sustainability Monitor with stochastic interest-growth differentials estimated from the history.
